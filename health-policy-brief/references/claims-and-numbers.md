# Claims, certainty and numbers

Basis: GRADE informative statements, CHEERS 2022, the IPCC uncertainty guide, trials on how uncertainty affects trust, and an audit of a local plan. The causal verb table and the basis-label rule are design choices.

## 1. Wording ladder

One rung per finding. Put the rung word and the number in the same sentence. Name the threshold or range the rating refers to.

| Certainty | Stem |
|---|---|
| High | "X reduces Y by about N." |
| Moderate | "X probably reduces Y by about N." (GRADE says "likely"; "probably" is a house choice) |
| Low | "X may reduce Y. We are not sure by how much." |
| Very low | "We do not know whether X changes Y. The evidence is too weak to say." |
| None found | "We found no study of X in a setting like yours." Write this only if a search was done and you can name it. If not: "The plan cites no study. We did not search." Never write it as "no effect", and never grade evidence "very low" when you have not seen any. |

Rules:

- Give a numeric range where data allow. A range barely lowers trust. "There is uncertainty" with no number lowers trust in the figure and its source.
- No "may" in a high-certainty sentence. No "will" in a low-certainty one.
- Print a key under the table. Symbols with a key helped lay readers more than "probably" or "may".
- Never show a bare confidence interval to a lay reader. Show the range in words: "between 3 and 9 fewer per 100".
- If you need probability words, attach numbers once: likely 66 to 100%, very likely 90 to 100%. Do not mix confidence (quality of evidence) and likelihood (probability) in one sentence.
- Give effects as absolute numbers beside relative ones, in a small table.

## 2. Scenarios, signposts and triggers

1. Label each projected number "Projection" or "Scenario", with the year.
2. Three scenarios at most, named by the assumption ("If vaccination stays at 62%").
3. Each scenario: assumption, date set, number it gives, action it leads to.
4. Signpost and trigger format: "Signpost: [indicator, source, who reports, how often]. Trigger: [value] by [date]. Then: [named action, named officer, money head]."
5. A good signpost is measurable, timely, reliable, accepted and has an owner.
6. Give a review date. Name the assumption that would force an earlier review.
7. Decide now only what holds in all scenarios.
8. Put model method, data years and fit in an annex. The main text says what the model cannot see.

Do not write "El Nino decays in 2027" or "no-regret" without a cited source and a scenario label.

## 3. Causal verbs by study design

| Design | Allowed | Banned |
|---|---|---|
| Randomised trial, high certainty | reduces, increases, prevents | proves |
| Systematic review of trials, moderate or low | probably or may reduce | shows, proves |
| Adjusted cohort or case-control | is associated with, was higher among | causes, leads to, drives, due to |
| Cross-sectional, survey, HMIS counts | is higher in, was found in | impact of, effect of, linked to cause |
| Before-after, no comparison | changed after, followed | impact, effect of, due to the scheme |
| Controlled before-after, interrupted time series | probably contributed to | achieved, delivered |
| Modelling | the model projects, under the assumption that | will, would save |
| Expert opinion, case study | experts report, we heard | evidence shows |

"Associated with" is read as causal by over half of raters. Avoiding the word is not enough. Say the real aim and how sure you are.

The recommendation is not stronger than the finding. If the design supports only association, write "worth trying and measuring", with a stop trigger.

Replace "studies show" with the count of studies, sample size and place. Six data points do not support a causal verb.

## 4. Basis label for every number and total

A basis label says what is counted, who counted it, the period, the unit, and what is left out.

- "Rs 42.3 crore total, FY 2026-27, State and Centre share, includes salaries, excludes buildings." (Example form only. Figures come from the user's files.)
- Show recurring and one-time costs on separate lines.
- Cost: state the perspective, price year, time horizon, and whether discounted.
- Range: same units, then central value, then what drives it.
- Baseline: name baseline, year and source.
- Say "percentage points" for differences between percentages. Show the denominator once per table.
- Show share missing and which districts.
- Two sources for one quantity (for example HMIS and a survey): show both, say which the decision uses, do not average.
- Totals add up, or say "may not add due to rounding".
- Two totals that readers will compare must be on the same basis. If they are not, label each, or give both ways.
- No basis, no total. Write "basis not provided" and list it as an open item.
- A money ask with no amount in the sources: write the ask without a figure. "Release the first tranche. Amount: to be confirmed by the Finance officer from the approved costing." Show the nearest sourced figure with its own basis label (for example the Phase 1 envelope), and say it is not the tranche amount. Never guess an amount.

## 5. Limits section

- Name only limits that could change the decision. At most five.
- Four parts each: what we could not see, why it matters, which way it could push the result, what would settle it.
- Put the weakest fact at the point of use. Add one "least certain part" line at the top.
- Key messages must not contradict the limits.
- Define every selected set ("the 16 selected districts": selected by what rule?).
- Mark each assumption and ask the officer to confirm it.

## 6. Local data

If the local number is missing, say so in the open-items list. Do not borrow a national number without saying it is national.
