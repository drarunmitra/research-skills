# Plain language rules

Honest note: sentence caps, grade targets and the active-voice rule are convention. No study shows they raise use. Intended plain language fails without a tool check: Cochrane plain summaries scored about Flesch 37 and fewer than half avoided jargon. So run the script and read the flags.

## Hard rules (the script checks most of them)

1. Open with the ask, the recommended action and the cost.
2. Define each technical term at first use. Add one line on what the evidence source is.
3. Expand each acronym at first use.
4. No sentence over 28 words. Split at 25.
5. No em dashes. No bold in running prose. Sentence case headings.
6. No bare confidence interval. No "there is uncertainty" without a number.
7. No banned causal verb for the study design.
8. Label opinion as opinion.
9. Numbered paragraphs and no bullets in Cabinet notes only.

## Soft rules (apply by default, adjust with a reason)

- Average sentence near 15 words. The "14 words" figure has no primary source.
- Paragraphs of 5 sentences or fewer.
- Active voice. Name who does what.
- Common words. "use" not "utilise". "start" not "commence". "help" not "facilitate".
- One idea per sentence. One instruction per sentence in action tables.
- Numbers in tables. Decision and reason in prose.
- Reading grade near 8 and Flesch near 60 as a flag only. The formulas count words, not meaning.
- Tailor to the role: a Minister needs the ask and the risk, a DM&HO needs the steps.

## Wording that comes from your sources

Do not copy a source's loose wording into the brief. "No-regret", "mixed monsoon" and "decays in 2027" are claims, not labels. Either drop them, or state the claim with its scenario label, range and citation. A source's own headings (for example "Key points for the Minister") may stay if the reader expects them.

## Action wording

Every action says: what, who, when, how much. "The DM&HO will issue the heat advisory to all PHCs by 15 March. Cost: Rs X lakh from [head]." Figures and heads come from the user.

## AI-tell patterns to avoid

Significance inflation ("crucial", "pivotal", "landscape"), filler openers, rule-of-three lists, reflexive hedging, and formulaic signposting. Use the `academic-writer` skill for the full pattern list. Run it last.

## Before and after

Before: "It is imperative to note that robust and multifaceted interventions will likely yield transformative outcomes for maternal health."

After: "We propose three actions. Two have evidence from trials. The third has not been tested in India. Together they may cut heat-related admissions among pregnant women. We do not know by how much."

Before: "Studies show the programme is effective."

After: "One trial of 2,400 women in [place] found [effect, range]. The certainty is low. We found no study in Telangana."


## Words to avoid or replace

Do not use: tranche (say "one payment"), firm or borderline for tests of a ranking (say "consistent" and "less certain"), re-runs (say "what-if runs"). Do not name a Minister or Secretary unless the user does. Ask the user to confirm any word you coined.
